Today I'm going to talk about the eucalyptus tree, this is a very common tree here in Australia, where it's also sometimes called the gum tree. 今天我要谈谈桉树，这是一种在澳大利亚非常常见的树，在这里有时也被称为胶树。⭐️【eucalyptus tree = 桉树】⭐️【gum tree = 胶树，桉树的俗称】
First, I'm going to talk about why it's important, then I'm going to describe some problems it faces at present. 首先，我会介绍它为什么重要，然后谈谈它目前面临的一些问题。
Right. Well, the eucalyptus tree is an important tree for lots of reasons. 好，桉树之所以重要，有很多原因。
For example, it gives shelter to creatures like birds and bats, and these and other species also depend on it for food, particularly the nectar from its flowers. 例如，它为鸟类和蝙蝠等动物提供庇护，而这些动物以及其他物种也依靠它获取食物，尤其是它花朵中的花蜜。⭐️【give shelter to = 为……提供庇护】⭐️【nectar = 花蜜】
So, it supports biodiversity. 因此，它有助于维持生物多样性。⭐️【support biodiversity = 维持生物多样性】
It's useful to us humans too, because we can kill germs with a disinfectant made from oil extracted from eucalyptus leaves. 它对我们人类也有用，因为我们可以用从桉树叶中提取的精油制成消毒剂，杀灭病菌。⭐️【disinfectant = 消毒剂】⭐️【extract … from … = 从……中提取……】
The eucalyptus grows all over Australia and the trees can live for up to 400 years. 桉树遍布澳大利亚，寿命可长达四百年。
So, it's alarming that all across the country numbers of eucalyptus are falling because the trees are dying off prematurely. 因此，令人担忧的是，澳大利亚各地的桉树数量都在减少，因为这些树正在过早地成批死亡。⭐️【alarming = 令人担忧的；令人警觉的】⭐️【die off = 相继死去；成批死亡】⭐️【prematurely = 过早地】
So, what are the reasons for this? 那么，造成这种情况的原因是什么呢？
One possible reason is disease. 一个可能的原因是疾病。
As far back as the 1970s, the trees started getting a disease called Mundulla Yellows, the trees leaves would gradually turn yellow, then the tree would die. 早在二十世纪七十年代，桉树就开始患上一种叫作“Mundulla黄化病”的疾病，树叶会逐渐变黄，随后整棵树死亡。⭐️【as far back as = 早在……，强调时间久远】
It wasn't until 2004 that they found the cause of the problem was lime or calcium hydroxide, to give it its proper chemical name, which was being used in the construction of roads. 直到2004年，人们才发现问题的根源是道路施工中使用的石灰，或者用它准确的化学名称来说，就是氢氧化钙。⭐️【It wasn't until … that … = 直到……才……】⭐️【lime = 石灰】⭐️【calcium hydroxide = 氢氧化钙】
The lime was being washed away into the ground and affecting the roots of the eucalyptus trees nearby. 石灰被水冲入土壤，影响了附近桉树的根部。⭐️【wash away = 冲走；冲刷带走】
What it was doing was preventing the trees from sucking up the iron they needed for healthy growth. 它所造成的影响，是阻碍桉树吸收健康生长所需的铁元素。⭐️【suck up = 吸收；吸入】
When this was injected back into the affected trees, they immediately recovered. 当铁元素被重新注入受影响的桉树后，它们便立即恢复了健康。⭐️【inject … into … = 将……注入……】
But this problem only affected a relatively small number of trees. 不过，这个问题只影响了相对少量的桉树。
By 2000, huge numbers of eucalyptus would dying along Australia's east coast of a disease known as "Bell-miner Associated Die-back". 到了2000年，澳大利亚东海岸已有大量桉树因一种被称为“钟吸蜜鸟相关枯梢病”的疾病而死亡。⭐️【die-back = 枯梢病；枝叶从末端开始枯死的现象】
The bell-miner is a bird and the disease seems to be common where there are high populations of bell-miners. 钟吸蜜鸟是一种鸟，而这种疾病似乎在钟吸蜜鸟数量较多的地区很常见。
Again, it's the leaves of the trees that are affected. 同样，受到影响的是树叶。
What happens is that insects settle on the leaves and eat their way round them, destroying them as they go. 具体情况是，昆虫落在树叶上，一边四处啃食，一边破坏树叶。⭐️【eat their way round = 一路啃食着绕行】⭐️【as they go = 在进行的过程中；一边……一边……】
And at the same time, they secrete a solution which has sugar in it. 与此同时，它们会分泌一种含糖的液体。⭐️【secrete = 分泌】⭐️【solution = 溶液】
The bell-miner birds really like this solution and in order to get as much as possible, they keep away other creatures that might try to get it. 钟吸蜜鸟非常喜欢这种液体，为了尽可能多地获取它，它们会赶走其他可能前来争食的动物。⭐️【keep away = 使……远离；不让……靠近】
So these birds and insects flourish at the expense of other species, and eventually so much damage is done to the leaves that the tree dies. 因此，这些鸟类和昆虫以牺牲其他物种为代价大量繁衍，最终树叶受损过于严重，导致整棵树死亡。⭐️【flourish = 繁盛；茁壮生长】⭐️【at the expense of = 以牺牲……为代价】
But experts say that trees can start looking sick before any sign of "Bell-miner Associated Die-back". 但专家表示，在出现任何“钟吸蜜鸟相关枯梢病”的迹象之前，桉树就可能已经开始显得不健康。
So it looks as if the problem might have another explanation. 因此，这个问题似乎可能还有另一种解释。
One possibility is that it's to do with the huge bushfires that we have in Australia. 一种可能是，这与澳大利亚发生的大规模林火有关。⭐️【be to do with = 与……有关】⭐️【bushfires = 林火；灌木林火灾】
A theory proposed over 40 years ago by ecologist William Jackson is that the frequency of bushfires in a particular region affects the type of vegetation that grows there. 生态学家William Jackson在四十多年前提出的一种理论认为，某个地区发生林火的频率，会影响当地生长的植被类型。⭐️【propose a theory = 提出一种理论】⭐️【vegetation = 植被】
If there are very frequent bushfires in a region, this encourages grass to grow afterwards. 如果一个地区的林火发生得非常频繁，就会促进火灾后草类的生长。
While, if the bushfires a rather less frequent, this results in the growth of eucalyptus forests. 而如果林火的发生频率稍低一些，就会促成桉树林的生长。⭐️【while = 而；用于对比两种情况】
So why is this? 那么，这是为什么呢？
Why do fairly frequent bushfires actually support the growth of eucalyptus. 为什么相当频繁的林火反而有利于桉树生长呢？
Well, one reason is that the fire stops the growth of other species which would consume water needed by eucalyptus trees. 一个原因是，火灾会阻止其他物种的生长，而这些物种原本会消耗桉树所需的水分。
And there's another reason, if these other quick growing species of bushes and plants are allowed to proliferate, they harm the eucalyptus in another way by affecting the composition of the soil and removing nutrients from it. 还有另一个原因：如果任由这些生长迅速的灌木和其他植物大量繁殖，它们还会通过改变土壤成分、消耗其中的养分，以另一种方式损害桉树。⭐️【proliferate = 迅速繁殖；大量增加】⭐️【the composition of the soil = 土壤的成分】⭐️【nutrients = 养分；营养物质】
So some bushfires are actually essential for the eucalyptus to survive as long as they are not too frequent. 因此，只要发生得不过于频繁，一定程度的林火实际上是桉树生存所必需的。⭐️【be essential for … to survive = 对……的生存必不可少】⭐️【as long as = 只要，引出条件】
In fact, there's evidence that Australia's indigenous people practised regular burning of bush land for thousands of years before the arrival of the Europeans. 事实上，有证据表明，在欧洲人到来之前的数千年里，澳大利亚原住民一直有定期焚烧灌木林地的做法。⭐️【indigenous people = 原住民】⭐️【practise = 实行；从事某种做法】
But since Europeans arrived on the continent, the number of bushfires has been strictly controlled. 但自从欧洲人来到这片大陆后，林火的发生次数就受到了严格控制。
Now scientists believe that this reduced frequency of bushfires to low levels has led to what is known as dry rainforest, which seems an odd name as usually we associate tropical rainforest with wet conditions. 如今，科学家认为，林火频率被降至较低水平，促成了所谓的“干旱雨林”。这个名称似乎有些奇怪，因为我们通常会把热带雨林与潮湿环境联系起来。⭐️【what is known as = 所谓的；被称为……的事物】⭐️【associate … with … = 将……与……联系起来】
And what special about this type of rainforest? 那么，这种雨林有什么特别之处呢？
Well, unlike tropical rainforest, which is a rich ecosystem, this type of ecosystem is usually a simple one. 热带雨林的生态系统十分丰富，而这种雨林与之不同，其生态系统通常比较简单。
It has very thick, dense vegetation, but not much variety of species. 它的植被非常茂密，但物种种类并不丰富。⭐️【dense vegetation = 茂密的植被】
The vegetation provides lots of shade, so one species that does find it ideal is the bell-miner bird, which builds its nests in the undergrowth there. 这些植被提供了大量荫蔽，因此，钟吸蜜鸟确实觉得这里是理想的栖息地，它们会在那里的林下灌丛中筑巢。⭐️【undergrowth = 林下灌丛；林下植被】
But again, that's not helpful for the eucalyptus tree. 但同样，这对桉树并没有好处。
