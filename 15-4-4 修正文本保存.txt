Hi everyone, in this session I'll be presenting my research about the social history of Britain during the Industrial Revolution. 大家好，这一节我将介绍自己对工业革命时期英国社会史的研究。
I particularly looked at how ordinary lives were affected by changes that happened at that time. 我重点研究了当时发生的变化如何影响普通人的生活。
This was a time that saw the beginning of a new phenomenon: consumerism – where buying and selling goods became a major part of ordinary people's lives. 这一时期出现了一种新现象：消费主义——商品买卖成为普通人生活中的重要组成部分。⭐️【consumerism = 消费主义】
In fact, it was in the 19th century that the quantity and quality of people's possessions was used as an indication of the wealth of the country. 事实上，正是在十九世纪，人们所拥有物品的数量和质量开始被用来衡量国家的富裕程度。⭐️【possessions = 个人拥有的物品；财物】
Before this, the vast majority of people had very few possessions, but all that was changed by the Industrial Revolution. 在此之前，绝大多数人拥有的物品很少，但工业革命改变了这一切。
This was the era from the mid-18th to the late 19th century, when improvements in how goods were made as well as in technology triggered massive social changes that transformed life for just about everybody in several key areas. 这一时期从十八世纪中叶持续到十九世纪末。其间，商品生产方式的改进和技术进步引发了大规模的社会变革，在几个关键领域改变了几乎所有人的生活。⭐️【trigger = 引发；触发】⭐️【just about = 几乎】
First let's look at manufacturing. 首先，我们来看看制造业。
When it comes to manufacturing, we tend to think of the Industrial Revolution in images of steam engines and coal. 谈到制造业，我们想到工业革命时，脑海中往往会浮现蒸汽机和煤炭的画面。
And it's true that the Industrial Revolution couldn't have taken place at all if it weren't for these new sources of power. 确实，如果没有这些新的动力来源，工业革命根本不可能发生。
They marked an important shift away from the traditional watermills and windmills that had dominated before this. 它们标志着动力来源发生了重大转变，逐渐摆脱了此前占主导地位的传统水磨和风车。⭐️【a shift away from = 从……转向其他方式；逐渐摆脱……】
The most advanced industry for much of the 19th century was textiles. 在十九世纪的大部分时间里，纺织业都是最先进的行业。⭐️【textiles = 纺织业；纺织品】
This meant that fashionable fabrics, and lace and ribbons were made available to everyone. 这意味着，时髦的布料、蕾丝和丝带变得人人都能买到。⭐️【lace = 蕾丝；花边】
Before the Industrial Revolution, most people made goods to sell in small workshops, often in their own homes. 工业革命之前，大多数人在小作坊里制作商品出售，而这些作坊往往就设在自己家中。
But enormous new machines were now being created that could produce the goods faster and on a larger scale, and these required a lot more space. 但当时，人们开始制造大型新式机器，能够更快、更大规模地生产商品，而这些机器需要大得多的空间。
So large factories were built, replacing the workshops, and forcing workers to travel to work. 因此，大型工厂建了起来，取代了小作坊，也迫使工人离家前往工作地点。
In fact, large numbers of people migrated from villages into towns as a result. 事实上，大量人口因此从乡村迁入城镇。
As well as manufacturing, there were new technologies in transport, contributing to the growth of consumerism. 除制造业外，交通运输领域也出现了新技术，推动了消费主义的发展。
The horse-drawn stagecoaches and carts of the 18th century, which carried very few people and goods, and travelled slowly along poorly surfaced roads, were gradually replaced by the numerous canals that were constructed. 十八世纪的驿站马车和货运马车，载客和载货量都很少，而且只能沿着路面条件差的道路缓慢行进。随着大量运河建成，这种运输方式逐渐被运河运输取代。⭐️【stagecoach = 驿站马车，旧时沿固定路线运行的公共马车】⭐️【poorly surfaced roads = 路面铺设条件差的道路】
These were particularly important for the transportation of goods. 这些运河对货物运输尤其重要。
The canals gradually fell out of use, though, as railways were developed, becoming the main way of moving goods and people from one end of the country to the other. 不过，随着铁路发展，运河逐渐不再使用，铁路成为全国范围内运输货物和旅客的主要方式。⭐️【fall out of use = 逐渐不再使用】
And the goods they moved weren't just coal, iron, clothes, and so on – significantly, they included newspapers, which meant that thousands of people were not only more knowledgeable about what was going on in the country, but could also read about what was available in the shops. 铁路运输的货物不仅包括煤炭、铁、衣服等——值得注意的是，其中还有报纸。这意味着，成千上万的人不仅更加了解国内正在发生的事情，还能通过阅读得知商店里有哪些商品。⭐️【significantly = 值得注意的是（话语功能：突出重要信息）】
And that encouraged them to buy more. 这又促使他们购买更多商品。
So faster forms of transport resulted in distribution becoming far more efficient – goods could now be sold all over the country, instead of just in the local market. 因此，更快捷的运输方式大幅提高了商品分销效率——商品如今能够销往全国，而不再局限于当地市场。⭐️【distribution = 商品分销；配送】
The third main area that saw changes that contributed to consumerism was retailing. 第三个通过自身变化推动消费主义发展的主要领域是零售业。⭐️【retailing = 零售业；零售活动】
The number and quality of shops grew rapidly, and in particular, small shops suffered as customers flocked to the growing number of department stores – a form of retailing that was new in the 19th century. 商店数量迅速增加，品质也不断提升。尤其是，随着顾客涌向数量日益增多的百货商店，小商店受到了冲击。百货商店是十九世纪新出现的一种零售形式。⭐️【flock to = 成群涌向】⭐️【department store = 百货商店】
The entrepreneurs who opened these found new ways to stock them with goods, and to attract customers: for instance, improved lighting inside greatly increased the visibility of the goods for sale. 开办百货商店的企业家找到了为商店备货和吸引顾客的新方法。例如，改善店内照明，使待售商品更容易被顾客看清。⭐️【stock something with = 为……备好／配齐货物】
Another development that made goods more visible from outside resulted from the use of plate glass, which made it possible for windows to be much larger than previously. 另一项变化是使用平板玻璃，使橱窗能够做得比以前大得多，也让店外的人更容易看见商品。⭐️【plate glass = 平板玻璃，尤指用于橱窗的大块厚玻璃】
New ways of promoting goods were introduced, too. 新的商品促销方式也出现了。
Previously, the focus had been on informing potential customers about the availability of goods; now there was an explosion in advertising trying to persuade people to go shopping. 以前，宣传的重点是告知潜在顾客有哪些商品可以买到；而现在，旨在说服人们购物的广告数量激增。⭐️【an explosion in = ……的激增】
Flanders claims that one of the great effects of the Industrial Revolution was that it created choice.&nbsp; Flanders认为，工业革命的一大影响就是为人们创造了选择。
All sorts of things that had previously been luxuries – from sugar to cutlery – became conveniences, and before long they'd turned into necessities: life without sugar or cutlery was unimaginable. Rather like mobile phones these days! 各种过去的奢侈品——从糖到餐具——先变成了让生活更便利的用品，不久又成了必需品：没有糖或餐具的生活变得难以想象。就像如今的手机一样！⭐️【cutlery = 刀、叉、匙等餐具】⭐️【conveniences = 使生活便利的用品或设施】⭐️【necessities = 生活必需品】
